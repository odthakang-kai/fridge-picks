What each one means
  • Wall to counter edge: back wall to the front edge of the countertop (about 25"). Shows how far it sticks out.
  • Wall to island: back wall to the island or counter across. Checks the doors open all the way.
  • Gap to side wall: only a wall or tall pantry on one side: gap from it to the fridge's side.
  • Nook depth: walls or tall cabinets on both sides: back wall to their front edge. The fridge sits forward so its doors clear them.

What these terms mean
Cubic feet (cu ft)
How much fits inside. A rough rule: about 4 to 5 cu ft per person, plus extra if you cook a lot or buy in bulk.
Counter depth
Shallower so the fridge sits closer to flush with your cabinets. Looks built in, but holds less than a standard-depth fridge of the same width.
Standard depth
Deeper, so it sticks out past the counters but holds more for the money.
French door
Two fridge doors on top, freezer drawer below. Fresh food at eye level, and each door needs less room to swing.
Zero-clearance hinges
The doors stay inside the fridge's width when they open, so it can sit right against a wall and the drawers still slide out.
Door swing
How far the doors reach into the room when open. Matters if there's an island or a narrow walkway.
Garage ready
Built to keep food at safe temperatures in a hot or cold garage, where regular fridges struggle.
Energy Star
Certified to use less electricity than the federal standard.
Spend More Save More
A store promotion that takes more off as the purchase total goes up. The "after promo" price shows it.
